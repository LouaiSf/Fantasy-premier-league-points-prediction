Purpose

Why this exists and who opens it.

The model predicts how many points each player will score in the coming gameweek. On its own that is a CSV. The site is how a decision gets made from it — which fifteen to own, which transfer is worth a −4, which week to burn a chip.

Three people open it, and they want different things:

  • An FPL manager wants an answer before the deadline: who to buy, who to captain, whether to hold.
  • A reviewer — a marker, an interviewer — wants to see that the model works and that its limits are understood.
  • Us, testing the model visually. A ranked CSV hides a bad prediction; a pitch view with a £4.0m unknown at centre-forward does not.

The gap worth closing first

Two trained classifiers are not surfaced anywhere. P(plays) at 0.954 AUC is the most accurate thing this project produces, and P(haul) at 0.869 answers the captaincy question directly. Both already exist in fpl_pipeline.ipynb. Building pages for them is wiring, not modelling — and it is the cheapest way to make the site show more of what the model does.

Built

Working today. Requirements listed are changes, not the whole feature.

Squad builder

Built

Best legal fifteen for a budget, solved as one integer program with the XI, bench order and captain decided together. Lock players in, exclude others, result drawn on a pitch.

  • SB-1Show a confidence band on each predicted score, not a bare number — ±1.0 is the measured error.
  • SB-2Warn when the optimal squad contains a player with no prior history.
  • SB-3Let the user save a solved squad to My Team in one click.

Transfers

Built

Best move at every transfer count, with the −4 hit charged before comparison, so a marginal second transfer shows as the loss it usually is.

  • TR-1Read the squad from My Team instead of the tab's own local copy.
  • TR-2Say explicitly when a gain is inside the model's error and the transfer is worth rolling.
  • TR-3Show the outgoing and incoming player side by side, with photos and the stats behind the swap.

Chips

Built

Bench Boost, Triple Captain, Free Hit and Wildcard ranked over a horizon, from fixture count, difficulty and how much of the user's squad plays.

  • CH-1Keep stating that per-player predictions do not vary by gameweek — the ranking is fixtures, not a projection.
  • CH-2Mark which chips the user has already played, read from their entry history.

Players & Watchlist

Built

Filterable grid of all 509 with photo, country flag, price, prediction and ownership. Watchlist surfaces value per million, differentials and overpriced players.

  • PL-1Add P(plays) to the card, once it is exposed by the API.
  • PL-2Add a compare mode — select two to four players, show their stats side by side.

To build

Requested. Both are backed by data already verified as available.

News

To buildHigh value

The model has no team-news feature, which is its single largest blind spot — it will happily rate a player who is out for three months. This page is the counterweight: every injury, suspension and doubt FPL has published, next to what the model thinks.

  • NW-1List every player with a non-empty news field — 180 of 655 today — newest first by news_added.
  • NW-2Show the playing chance as a percentage with the raw note: "Groin injury — 25% chance of playing".
  • NW-3Flag conflicts loudly: a player the model rates highly who has a 0% chance is the case this page exists to catch.
  • NW-4Highlight anyone in the user's squad, at the top, separated from the rest.
  • NW-5Filter by status: injured, suspended, doubtful, transferred out of the league.
  • NW-6Show when the data was fetched. Stale team news is worse than none.

My Team

To buildHigh value

One place the user's squad lives, instead of a list retyped on the Transfers tab. Entering fifteen names by hand is the worst interaction in the current site; importing by FPL ID removes it entirely.

  • MT-1Import by FPL manager ID via /api/entry/{id}/event/{gw}/picks/ — pulls the real fifteen, captain, vice and bench order.
  • MT-2Show manager name, overall rank, total points and bank from /api/entry/{id}/.
  • MT-3Keep manual entry as a fallback, through the existing player picker.
  • MT-4Persist the ID so the import is automatic on the next visit.
  • MT-5Draw the squad on a pitch with each player's prediction, and total the XI.
  • MT-6Score the squad against the optimal one at the same value, and name the weakest slots.
  • MT-7Become the single source for Transfers, Chips and News — enter the squad once.
Explain the ID

Most people do not know their manager ID. Say where to find it: it is the number in the URL when viewing your own points page on the FPL site.

Proposed

Ordered by value for effort. The first two are the strongest: both surface a model that already exists and currently appears nowhere.

Captain picker

ProposedHigh valueLow effort

Expected points and upside are different questions. A steady five-point midfielder and a forward who returns 2 or 15 can carry the same prediction, and only one is a captain. The P(haul) classifier answers this directly at 0.869 AUC and is not used anywhere.

  • CP-1Rank the user's XI by P(≥10 points), shown as a percentage against the 1.7% base rate.
  • CP-2Show expected points beside it — when the two disagree, that is the interesting case.
  • CP-3Be honest that in backtesting this did not beat ranking by expected points; it is a second opinion, not a better one.

Will they play?

ProposedHigh valueLow effort

The play classifier reaches 0.954 AUC — the most accurate output this project has. Combined with the News page it answers the question that decides most FPL outcomes: is this player starting.

  • WP-1Show P(plays) on every player card and in My Team.
  • WP-2Flag squad members below 50% before the deadline.
  • WP-3Show the model's own estimate next to FPL's published chance — they are independent, and disagreement is a signal.

Fixture ticker

ProposedMedium valueLow effort

A grid of the next six to eight gameweeks, one row per club, each cell coloured by difficulty. The most-used tool in the FPL community, and the fixture data is already loaded for the Chips page.

  • FT-1Sort clubs by total difficulty over the window, easiest first.
  • FT-2Mark blank and double gameweeks distinctly — they drive chip decisions.
  • FT-3Highlight the clubs the user owns.

How it works

ProposedMedium valueLow effort

A model card. For a reviewer this may be the most important page on the site: it shows the numbers are honest and the limits are understood.

  • HW-1Per-position R² and MAE, against the rolling-average baseline, on the held-out seasons.
  • HW-2Explain the season-level holdout and why a random split would be wrong here.
  • HW-3Show the backtest: the picked XI beat the baseline in 29 of 33 gameweeks, +481 points.
  • HW-4State the limits plainly — no team news, ±1.0 error, chip timing is fixtures rather than a projection.
  • HW-5Name what was measured and dropped. Expected goals adding nothing is a finding worth showing.

Price watch

ProposedMedium valueMedium effort

Prices move on net transfers, and a rise or fall changes what a squad can afford. The pipeline already computes price momentum; the transfer counts are in the bootstrap payload.

  • PW-1Rank likely risers and fallers by net transfers this gameweek.
  • PW-2Flag squad members about to fall, and targets about to rise.
  • PW-3Call this a heuristic, not a model — FPL's real algorithm is undisclosed.

Gameweek review

ProposedLower valueMedium effort

After the gameweek: what the model predicted against what happened. The most credible thing a prediction site can do is show its own misses.

  • GR-1Predicted against actual for the user's squad, with the week's error.
  • GR-2The model's biggest hits and misses league-wide.
  • GR-3A running accuracy line across the season, so drift is visible.

Wildcard planner

ProposedLower valueLow effort

A wildcard is the squad builder with no transfer limit, which the site can already solve. What it needs is framing: build from scratch, compare against what is owned, and count how many moves the switch would take.

  • WC-1Solve a fresh fifteen at the user's squad value plus bank.
  • WC-2Show the diff against the current squad, and the predicted gain.
  • WC-3Optimise over a multi-gameweek horizon, not just the next one.

Applies to every page

Non-functional requirements. The first is the one most easily lost.

  • NF-1Never show a prediction as a fact. Measured error is ±1.0 points per player. Gaps smaller than that are noise and the interface should say so rather than implying a ranking is meaningful.
  • NF-2Players with no prior history are flagged everywhere they appear. Their predictions come from zero-filled features — an absence of evidence, not a forecast.
  • NF-3Every page states when its data was fetched, and from where.
  • NF-4A solve returns in under a second. It is 0.3 s today; keep it there.
  • NF-5Works at 400px. Tables scroll inside their own container; the page never scrolls sideways.
  • NF-6Light and dark both designed, not inverted.
  • NF-7API failure degrades to local data with a visible notice, never a blank page or a silent stale number.
  • NF-8Every figure traces to the same functions the CLI calls. Two code paths computing one number is how this project's worst bugs happened.

Out of scope

  • Making transfers. The API is read-only without authentication, and handling FPL credentials is not worth it.
  • Mini-league tables. Available, but it is a social feature and shows nothing about the model.
  • Live in-play scores. A different product, with a polling cost this does not need.
  • Accounts. A manager ID in local storage is enough; nothing here needs a password.

Open questions

  • Does My Team replace the Transfers squad entry outright, or shadow it until the import is trusted?
  • Does News stand alone, or fold into Players as a filter? Standalone is more visible; a filter is less to build.
  • How stale may cached bootstrap data get before the site refuses to show it — an hour, a day, until the next deadline?
  • Is the site deployed anywhere, or run locally? Deployment changes the caching and rate-limit answers substantially.
